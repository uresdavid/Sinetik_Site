01

Training a person, not a profile

Most programmes are written for an average athlete: a standard body, a standard week, a standard level of fatigue. That athlete does not exist.

You have your own bone levers, your own spontaneous way of producing force, a week that looked nothing like the last one, a match on Sunday and perhaps a back that has been pulling since February. A plan that ignores all of this always ends the same way: a plateau, pain that returns to the same place, or the feeling of working hard for nothing.

Our position is simple. We do not organise training around what you are, but around what we decide to bring about — today, with the body that turns up today. Your natural way of moving does not become a label: it guides the choice of exercises and the way we coach you through the effort.

One coach handles the whole thing, from the assessment to the follow-up. You never change contact halfway through.

Before every session

Two questions, always the same

Two non-negotiable conditions before loading anything. They take thirty seconds, and they change the session.

  • Available energy. What you actually have today, not what the plan assumed three weeks ago. The session is kept, lightened or replaced.
  • Pain, from 0 to 10. Below 5, we work on the specific. Above it, we work on something else — and we look for the reason.

This isn't leniency. It is what makes a plan last six months instead of three weeks.

03

The method

Four stages, always in this order. The order does the work, not the exercise catalogue.

  1. Read before programming Your history, your injuries, your real week — and the way you spontaneously produce force. Some people organise through anchoring and ground contact, others through rebound and lightness. Neither is better: it is a starting point, and it changes both the exercises we choose and the way we coach you.
  2. Build what protects Strength, movement quality, the capacity to repeat effort. The least spectacular part, and the best documented. Everything else rests on it — and without it, everything else eventually gives way.
  3. Make it specific, gradually We bring the work closer to the real demands of your sport and your position as the deadline approaches. Gradually: specificity too early is the most expensive shortcut in physical preparation.
  4. Adjust, then start again We measure the real effect, we re-test, we decide what comes next. Then we go back to the beginning, one notch finer. The plan serves the athlete, never the other way round: it changes when your week changes.
One session = one intention

Four intentions, never five objectives

Every session pursues one intention and one only. That is what makes it readable for you and assessable for us. Three priority objectives per cycle at most: anyone chasing five at once makes serious progress on none.

  • Activate — prepare the system to work: wake it up and connect it, without tightening.
  • Soften — release what is blocking: slowly, consciously, with the breath.
  • Stabilise — build a reliable base: support, control, robustness.
  • Perform — express and develop the quality: the right exercise, at the right dose, at the right moment.

Never Perform first. A system that is neither available nor stable does not turn effort into progress — it turns it into wear.

05

What we hold ourselves to

Three things we don't compromise on. They occasionally cost us a client. They always save us a bad piece of coaching.

  1. We don't programme without assessing first If you only want a plan, we can give you one — but we'll call it a generic plan, not individual coaching.
  2. We don't keep a worrying signal to ourselves If something in what we see concerns us, we tell you, we document it and we refer you on. Even if there's a match on Sunday.
  3. We won't tell you a method is proven when it isn't You trust us with your body and your time. You are entitled to know what we base our work on — and above all where the established evidence stops.

Levels of evidence, said out loud

Few pages publish this. We would rather you read it here than discover it in a session. Here are the approaches we use, ranked by how solid the evidence is.

Foundation

Solidly documented

Strength development, periodisation, high-intensity work, training-load management, progressive return-to-play criteria, growth monitoring in young athletes.

This is the foundation, and it is most of what we will do with you.

Complement

Documented, but debated

Profiling from vertical-jump data to guide load. Reading motor preferences — producing force through anchoring or through rebound. Research exists; consensus does not.

We use it to guide choices, never to make a diagnosis.

Exploratory

Still to be demonstrated

Integrating primitive reflexes in adult athletes. The literature is solid in children, thin in adult sport.

We use it occasionally, as a complement, and we flag it every time we touch it.

06

Questions we get asked

  1. Does this replace the work at my club? No, and it isn't meant to. What we do comes alongside it, in slots that don't compete with team sessions. When it helps, we tell the club.
  2. How long before something shows? The first changes in how you feel often come within three to four weeks. Measurable change — strength, speed, the capacity to repeat effort — takes two to three months of consistent work. We don't promise a percentage at eight weeks.
  3. Do I need a gym? For remote coaching, no: the programming adapts to the equipment you have. You do need to be able to film yourself, otherwise we're working blind.
  4. I'm coming back from injury. When can I start? Once your physiotherapist gives the go-ahead. We take over, we don't take their place, and we stay in touch with them if you'd like.
  5. What if this isn't right for me? We'll tell you in the first conversation, and point you towards something that suits you better. Badly matched coaching costs both sides time.
