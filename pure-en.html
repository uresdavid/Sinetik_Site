01

The problem nobody looks at

You've gone half a size down "to feel the ball better." You know the black toenail in November, the blister on the little toe, the burn under the forefoot after a match on dry ground. On a pitch, these things have become normal. They shouldn't be.

The foot is not just a platform. It is an organ of perception: thousands of receptors constantly feed balance, loading and coordination. Locked inside a narrow, heavily wrapped boot, it sends back impoverished information. Footwear that is too short or too tight is also associated in the literature with subungual haematoma, toe deformities and Morton's neuroma.

In a growing player this matters: the shape of the adult foot is built between roughly 10 and 18, inside the boot worn five times a week.

And that narrowness has no technical justification. It comes from aesthetic legacy and speed marketing — not from biomechanical optimisation.

Our design principles

Six decisions, and the reason behind each. No technology invented for the story.

01

A wide forefoot

The shape follows real anatomy: the toes keep the room to spread as the foot loads, the way they do barefoot.

02

A locked heel

This is where hold is won — structured counter, central lacing. The foot doesn't float over the studs. Wide at the front does not mean loose.

03

A plate that works

Pebax from the first model, stiffness tuned by zone: firm enough to transmit energy, flexible enough for a natural roll.

04

Studs that let you turn

A conical-dominant stud map, designed to let the foot pivot rather than lock into the ground. Firm ground, natural grass.

05

Minimal stack

No thick midsole. Comfort comes from shape, footbed and flex — not from a stack of foam that cuts you off from the ground.

06

An honest lifespan

A boot that lasts two seasons beats a recycled boot that lasts six months. Removable, replaceable footbed, compatible with your orthotics.

03

What we don't promise you.

Plenty of brands will swear their boot makes you play better. We won't.

There is no solid evidence that a wide forefoot improves ball touch. We could have sold you that anyway. We won't. And no boot prevents injury — the ones that claim otherwise are lying to you.

What we stand behind is what the literature actually supports: a foot that keeps its function, a geometry that respects your anatomy, and comfort that lets you play without thinking about your feet.

The rest — the speed, the touch, the goals — is still your job.
